Rules for authoring a CV for a given job

Read the posting first, then score the fit on the role page, then write. The CV is a one-page plain document aimed at three readers, in order: the applicant tracking system, the recruiter (about six seconds), and the hiring manager. A CV that fails an earlier reader never reaches the next one.

1. Exactly one page

US Letter, one page. Open the role page’s Print 1-page view and confirm a single page before sending anything. When it spills onto page two, cut in this order: the oldest roles, then preferred-only keywords, then extra bullets on roles that do not serve the posting’s top three requirements. The most recent relevant roles keep three to five bullets. Older roles shrink to a title line and one bullet, or leave the page.

Keep the type at about 10.5pt or larger and the margins at about 0.6 inches. Contact information counts toward the page. Patents, when they matter for this job, are a few lines on page one. A second page titled “additional patents” does not go out.

2. Make it readable by an ATS

The file an employer receives is a single column of text. In that file, use ordinary paragraphs and plain bullets. Leave out tables, columns, text boxes, sidebars, images, icons, graphics, skill bars, and text inside shapes. This authoring page may use tables. The CV text may not.

  • Section headings, in this order, skipping a section only when it is empty or irrelevant to the posting: Summary, Experience, Skills, Education, Patents.
  • A standard face: Calibri, Arial, Cambria, or Times New Roman.
  • Plain bullets, a hyphen or a round bullet. Dates written Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY with an en dash. A current role ends with Present.
  • Contact information as the first lines of the document body: name, city, email, phone if Richard has confirmed one, LinkedIn, and the public portfolio URL. Word and PDF headers and footers are dropped by many parsers, so the contact block lives in the body.
  • Deliver a .docx or a text-based PDF exported from a document (the text must be selectable). The Download .txt button is for drafting and for pasting into that document.
  • File name: Richard-Bailey-Company-Role.docx. ASCII, hyphens, no date-stamp clutter.

3. Mirror the posting’s must-have words

Copy the must-have keywords and the job title into the keyword table before drafting. Where a fact in resume.json is truly the same thing, use the posting’s wording. If the posting says “spatial computing,” the CV says spatial computing where that work exists, alongside any more specific term Richard actually uses. Preferred keywords go in only when they are true and only when they do not push a must-have off the page.

Every keyword needs a real line that shows where it was used: a bullet, the summary, or the skills line. A skills list that repeats the posting with no matching experience is keyword stuffing, and hiring managers notice it. Anything still missing stays in the keyword table as missing, with a one-line reason.

4. Put the top three requirements in the top third

The first third of the page is the name, the contact lines, the target title, the summary, and the opening of the most relevant role. Those lines answer the posting’s top three requirements in the posting’s words.

The summary is three or four lines: the target role, the real scope (years, platforms, or team size that resume.json supports), the three requirements, and one proof point that includes a number already in the data. Experience stays reverse-chronological. When the strongest proof sits in an older role, name that company and result in the summary so a recruiter sees it immediately, then give that role enough bullets for a hiring manager to find the detail.

5. Write outcome-first bullets, and spend space where it counts

Lead with the result, then what Richard did, then the scope. “Cut SDK build times 70% by prioritizing technical debt and expanding CI across iOS and Android” is the shape. “Responsible for CI” is not. Numbers come from resume.json (the impact fields and the achievement text) or from a confirmation written in the role page’s notes. When a number is not there, describe the outcome without inventing a figure, or ask Richard and wait.

Give the most lines to the most recent role and to the roles that match this posting. Condense everything else. One idea per bullet. Open with a verb. Team size, title, and dates match the experience entry.

6. Win the recruiter’s six-second scan

A recruiter reads the title line, the summary, the company names, and the most recent title. The title line is the posting’s title when Richard can honestly wear it, or the closest true title from resume.json when the posting’s title would overclaim. The summary uses that same title and the top must-have terms. The first bullet of the most relevant role should stand alone, so a recruiter can repeat it. Company names stay the real names recorded in the data.

7. Give the hiring manager depth

After the scan, the hiring manager looks for scope, architecture, ownership, and what shipped. On the roles that match, say what Richard owned (the architecture, the API, the launch, the team), the shape of the system, and the result that reached users or developers. Include tools when they support that story. A tool list with no ownership does not answer a hiring manager.

Where a role is both leadership and hands-on, show both, in the order this posting cares about. Quintar is stored as technical management and individual contributor, title “Vice President of Engineering & Principle Engineer.” Niantic Lightship and Magic Leap are senior-director scope with shipped platform results. Use that depth. For an IC posting, lead with what he designed and shipped. For a leadership posting, lead with the team, the bar, and the launch, still with a technical result.

8. Use only confirmed facts

  • The source of truth is src/data/resume.json: profile, experiences, skills, patents, learning, and publications. Anything Richard confirms for this application is written in the role page notes before it enters the CV.
  • Employers, titles, dates, metrics, patents, and tools stay as recorded. The Quintar title is stored as “Principle Engineer.” Use that spelling until Richard confirms a different external spelling on the role page.
  • Dates come from startDate and endDate. A current role (current: true, null end date) is written through Present. Quintar starts September 2022.
  • There is no education record in resume.json. The intro line “BS/MS in Computer Science.” is an Apple posting requirement stored in profile.intro, not a degree entry. Education goes on a CV only after Richard confirms school, degree, and year, and that confirmation is in the notes.
  • publications is empty. Add a publication only when Richard confirms the citation.
  • If a bullet needs a fact that is not in the data, leave it out of the CV and record the question in notes.

9. Count Apple-platform work toward iOS requirements, and name the real platform

iOS, visionOS / Apple Vision Pro, Swift, SwiftUI, RealityKit, ARKit, and Xcode / Instruments work all count when a posting asks for iOS or Apple-platform experience. Say what the work actually was. Vision Pro work is visionOS. App work that resume.json places on iOS is iOS. Both can sit in one truthful sentence, for example the Quintar deliveries already recorded as PGA Tour Vision on Apple Vision Pro and NASCAR, MLB, NBA, and FIFA on iOS, Android, and Meta Quest, built with Swift, SwiftUI, and RealityKit.

In the keyword table, an iOS must-have can be covered or partial on the strength of that Apple-platform evidence. The CV still names visionOS, Vision Pro, or Swift. It does not retitle that work as an iOS role or swap a visionOS bullet into an iOS bullet. When the posting specifically requires UIKit App Store apps and the data only supports visionOS or Swift, mark the keyword partial and say so in the fit score.

10. Leave confidential and unapproved names out

Do not name unapproved partner products. Snap SPECS is the standing example: it does not appear on a CV, in an outreach note, or in interview stories. Keep Quintar’s confidential detail out as well: unreleased customers, unannounced commercial terms, and internal architecture that is not already written in resume.json. Shipments already in the resume data may be used. Anything past that waits for Richard’s yes, recorded in the notes. The public portfolio URL may appear in the contact block. The /cover-cv/ URL never appears on a CV or in outreach.

11. Answer the hiring manager’s likely concerns on the page

Before drafting, write the objections this hiring manager is likely to have, each with the mitigation that will show up in the CV or the outreach note. Use a concern only when this posting invites it.

  • VP or Senior Director applying to an IC role. The worry is that he will not write code or will not take technical direction. Frame the management years as hands-on technical leadership: architecture he set, code-level performance work, and what shipped. Lead the recent bullets with the engineering, and keep the team size as scope rather than as a request to be hired as a VP.
  • A narrow “N years of iOS” line next to a career of spatial platforms. Count Apple-platform years honestly under rule 9. State the platform. Do not inflate an iOS-only year count by renaming other work.
  • A domain shift, such as live sports AR toward the posting’s domain. Lead with the transferable system (real-time graphics, spatial registration, performance, developer platforms) and use the posting’s domain words where the work actually transferred.

If a concern cannot be answered with a confirmed fact, lower the fit score. A score of 8–10 means the must-haves are covered with real evidence. A score of 5–7 means one real gap with a mitigation. A score of 1–4 means pass, unless Richard explicitly overrides it in the notes.

12. Checklist before applying

  • Print preview is a single US Letter page, black text on white, and the text is selectable.
  • Contact information is in the body, and the document has no header, footer, table, column, text box, image, or icon.
  • Headings are Summary, Experience, Skills, and, when they belong, Education and Patents.
  • Dates use Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY. The target title and the summary match the role in the first screen.
  • The top three requirements appear in the top third, in the posting’s words.
  • Every must-have and preferred keyword is marked covered, partial, or missing.
  • Every employer, title, date, and number traces to resume.json or to a confirmation in the notes.
  • Apple-platform work counted toward iOS is still named as iOS, visionOS, Vision Pro, or Swift, whichever it was.
  • Snap SPECS and other unapproved partner products are absent. Quintar confidential detail is absent. The cover-cv URL is absent.
  • Each hiring-manager concern has a mitigation in the CV or in the outreach note.
  • The outreach note is under about 120 words and adds no claim the CV does not already make.
  • Two or three STAR stories on the role page map to the top requirements, using the same facts as the CV.
  • The file sent is .docx or a text-based PDF named Richard-Bailey-Company-Role.
  • This index row shows the company, role, status, and date.
